the scheme moves the bill by $5.54bn (down) and the resource cost by $0.87bn (up).
the difference, $6.41bn, is a transfer: more capacity lowers the pool
price, which moves money from generators to consumers without saving any of it.
A comparison that showed only the bill would report the transfer as a benefit.
of that resource-cost move, $0.51bn is outage the scheme avoided
and -$1.38bn is fuel, fixed costs and capital: what got built
instead of what would have been. The two add to the move above, and a negative
figure ran against it.
unserved energy is valued at the market price cap of $20,300/MWh. That is
a regulatory figure standing in for what an outage costs, not a measurement of one.

One seed, and not much of it travels. Run the same comparison on another weather
sequence and both the outage line and the cost line can change sign. Neither this
run's outage nor its total is therefore a property of the scheme, and neither should
be quoted as one. What travels better is the sort: the outage avoided is largest on
the fast-growing draws and smallest on the slow ones, where the scheme buys capacity
that has little to do. tools/ten_seeds.py runs the envelope and decomposes it, and a
copy sits under outputs/canonical/ten_seeds.csv.

A stylised fleet. Illustrative, and not a forecast of anything.
