the scheme moves the BILL by $21.06bn (down) and the RESOURCE COST by $0.40bn (up).
the difference, $21.46bn, is a TRANSFER: more capacity lowers the pool
price, which moves money from generators to consumers without saving any of it.
A comparison that showed only the bill would report the transfer as a benefit.
of that resource-cost move, $2.07bn is outage the scheme avoided
and $2.47bn is fuel, fixed costs and capital: what got built
instead of what would have been.
unserved energy is valued at the market price cap of $20,300/MWh. That is
a regulatory figure standing in for what an outage costs, not a measurement of one.

ONE SEED, AND NOT MUCH OF IT TRAVELS. Across ten weather sequences the scheme
improves reliability in six and worsens it in four, and lowers the total resource
cost in four. Both lines change sign across the seeds, so neither this run's outage
nor its total is a property of the scheme. What does travel is the sort: the outage
avoided is largest on the fast-growing draws and nil on the slow ones, where the
scheme buys capacity that has nothing to do. tools/ten_seeds.py writes and
decomposes the envelope, and a copy sits under outputs/canonical/.

A stylised fleet. Illustrative, and not a forecast of anything.
